# Evidence and coverage

## Build a coverage map

Before composing slides, account for every supplied fact, requirement, chart, example, and caveat. Keep a working map such as:

| Source item | Narrative role | Destination | Evidence state | Locator | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three connected sources | Proof of progress | Progress slide | Reported | supplied brief | placed |
| Target of 10–12 | Direction | Progress slide | Reported target | supplied brief | placed |
| Portfolio drill-down | Capability | Demonstration slide | Illustrative values | user request | placed |

Complete the map before declaring the deck content-complete. Add slides when material cannot fit without damaging legibility; do not silently drop content to preserve a preferred slide count.

## Evidence states

Use consistent language:

- **Verified** — supported by an authoritative source or inspected artifact.
- **Derived** — mechanically calculated from verified inputs.
- **Reported** — supplied by a stakeholder or secondary document.
- **Target** — intended future state, not current performance.
- **Illustrative** — invented solely to demonstrate a visual or interaction.
- **Unknown** — material information not supplied.

Place the state where the audience encounters the claim. An appendix disclaimer does not adequately qualify an illustrative chart shown earlier.

## Presentation-level sourcing

- Preserve exact dates, units, denominators, and comparison periods.
- Put compact source lines on evidence slides when locators exist.
- Distinguish current, live, in progress, planned, target, and future scope.
- Show uncertainty when it affects the decision; do not create false precision.
- If sources conflict, resolve the conflict with the user or present the discrepancy explicitly.

## Coverage audit

Before delivery, confirm:

- every required source item has a destination or an explicit exclusion reason;
- every number has a state and unit;
- every visual claim is supported by the encoded data or mechanism;
- every illustrative element is labelled at first use;
- no summary claim exceeds the evidence available;
- the close follows from the evidence shown rather than introducing a new unsupported conclusion.
